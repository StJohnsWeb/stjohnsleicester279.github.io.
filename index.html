A Leicester lodge with deep roots

St John's Lodge No. 279 is a Craft lodge under the United Grand Lodge of England and part of the Provincial Grand Lodge of Leicestershire and Rutland. We meet at Freemasons' Hall, 80 London Road, a short walk from Leicester railway station.

Freemasonry is one of the world's oldest social and charitable organisations. It brings together men of every background who share a belief in integrity, kindness, honesty and fairness. Membership is open to any man over 18, regardless of race, religion or background.

Our members today include business owners, tradespeople, teachers, engineers, health workers and retirees from across Leicester and Leicestershire.

Friendship

Lifelong friendships built across generations, professions and faiths.

The history of St John's Lodge

The story of St John's is woven into the story of Leicester itself, from Georgian coaching inns on the Market Place to Freemasons' Hall on London Road.

Freemasonry first arrived in Leicester in 1739, but the town's early lodges did not survive. St John's Lodge was warranted in 1790 and, for over three decades after 1813, was the only lodge working in the town. It is the oldest lodge still meeting in the Province of Leicestershire and Rutland.

For its first seventy years the lodge met in Leicester's inns: the Lion and Dolphin and the White Swan on the Market Place, the Three Cranes on Gallowtree Gate, the George in the Haymarket and the Bell Hotel on Humberstone Gate. In 1859 its members helped fund a dedicated Freemasons' Hall on Halford Street, independent of any tavern, and in 1910 the lodge moved to Freemasons' Hall at 80 London Road, where it still meets.

  1. 1790Warrant granted on 31 August by the "Moderns" Grand Lodge. The lodge is consecrated on 11 November at the Lion and Dolphin, Market Place.
  2. 1813The Moderns and Antients unite to form the United Grand Lodge of England. St John's becomes No. 525.
  3. 1846A second Leicester lodge, John of Gaunt, is consecrated, ending more than thirty years in which St John's worked alone in the town.
  4. 1801–1821The lodge moves between the Three Cranes on Gallowtree Gate, the White Swan, the George in the Haymarket and the Bell Hotel on Humberstone Gate.
  5. 1859Freemasons' Hall opens on Halford Street, with St John's brethren helping to fund it.
  6. 1863The lodge is renumbered to its present number, No. 279.
  7. 1890Centenary celebrations and the award of a Centenary Warrant.
  8. 1910The lodge moves to Freemasons' Hall, 80 London Road.
  9. 1990Bicentenary.

Notable brethren of St John's Lodge

Over two centuries, members of St John's Lodge itself have included merchants, manufacturers, architects, civic leaders and historians who helped build the Leicester we know today, from the gift of Bradgate Park to the fountain in Town Hall Square.

Historian & civic figure

William Kelly

Initiated into St John's in 1838, Kelly served as Deputy Provincial Grand Master from 1852 and became Provincial Grand Master in 1870. His history of the Provincial Grand Lodge, published that year, remains a key source on Freemasonry in the county.

Nobleman & Master of the Lodge

Earl Howe

Earl Howe served as Worshipful Master of St John's and went on to lead the whole county as Provincial Grand Master until 1870, when he was succeeded by fellow St John's man William Kelly.

Who can become a Freemason in Leicester?

Any man aged 18 or over who believes in a Supreme Being, whatever his religion, race or background, and who wants to be part of a supportive community.

How do I join St John's Lodge?

Get in touch using the form below. A member will arrange an informal chat, usually over a coffee or a meal, so you can ask questions and meet some of the brethren.

Is Freemasonry a secret society?

No. Freemasonry is open about its aims, its meeting places and its charitable work. Like many organisations, it keeps some traditional ceremonies private to members.
