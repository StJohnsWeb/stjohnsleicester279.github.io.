Sir Israel Hart and the Town Hall Square fountain

Anyone who has sat beside the fountain in Town Hall Square has enjoyed the generosity of Israel Hart. A successful manufacturer and long-serving councillor, he gave Leicester one of its best-loved landmarks, and he was also a member of St John's Lodge.

A Leicester merchant

Hart was chairman of Hart & Levy, a wholesale clothing manufacturing firm, at a time when Leicester's textile and clothing trades were booming. His business success went hand in hand with a long commitment to the town's public life.

Four times Mayor of Leicester

Hart was first elected to the town council in 1874 and served as a councillor for around 25 years. He was made an alderman and elected Mayor in 1884, re-elected in 1885 and 1886, and chosen again in 1893. He was the first Jewish man to hold the office of Mayor of Leicester, and he also served as High Bailiff of the borough. He was knighted in 1895.

The Town Hall Square fountain

Leicester's new Town Hall opened in 1876 on the site of the old cattle market. In 1878 Hart, then a councillor, offered the borough an ornamental fountain for the open ground in front of it, and the council accepted his gift on 29 October that year.

The fountain and the layout of the square were designed by Francis Hames, the architect of the Town Hall itself. Built from bronze-painted cast iron with Shap and Ross of Mull granite, it features four winged lions around a columned pedestal rising to two tiered bowls. Hart unveiled it on 24 September 1879. Today it is Grade II listed and remains a favourite meeting place in the city centre.

A strikingly similar fountain stands in Porto, Portugal. It was long said that Hames copied the Porto design, but the Leicester fountain is the earlier of the two, suggesting the foundry may have reused the Leicester pattern.

Other gifts to the town

As well as the fountain, Hart presented Leicester with a free library. In 1900 he stood for Parliament as a Liberal in Central Hackney.
