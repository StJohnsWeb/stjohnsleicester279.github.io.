Charles Bennion: the man who gave Bradgate Park to Leicestershire

Engineer, shoe machinery pioneer and one of Leicester's greatest benefactors, and a member of St John's Lodge No. 279.

Born
1857
Died
21 March 1929, aged 71
Home
Thurnby Grange, Thurnby

Lodge
St John's Lodge No. 279

Few people have left a gift to Leicestershire as lasting as Charles Bennion's. Every year, families walking among the deer and the ruins of Bradgate House are enjoying a park that exists for the public because of one Leicester industrialist, and a member of St John's Lodge.

From ship's engineer to Leicester industrialist

Bennion trained as an apprentice at the Crewe railway works before travelling the world as a ship's engineer. On returning to England he moved into shoe machinery manufacture and, in the 1880s, settled in Leicester, which alongside Northampton was one of the great centres of Britain's boot and shoe trade.

His firm, Pearson & Bennion of Belgrave, grew into one of the country's leading suppliers of shoe-making machinery by the mid-1890s.

Founding British United Shoe Machinery

In 1899 Bennion travelled to Boston to negotiate a merger with the newly formed United Shoe Machinery Company of America. The result was the British United Shoe Machinery Company, incorporated in October 1899 and headquartered at the Union Works on Belgrave Road. Bennion led the company as managing director from its formation until his death.

For most of the 20th century the company was the largest manufacturer of footwear machinery in the world, exporting to more than 50 countries and employing thousands of Leicester people. A blue plaque marks the former site on Belgrave Road.

The gift of Bradgate Park

Bradgate Park had been a deer park for centuries, home to the Grey family and famous as the childhood home of Lady Jane Grey. When its future was uncertain in the 1920s, Bennion stepped in. With the agreement of the Grey heirs, he bought the park in 1928 and placed it in trust for the city and county of Leicester, so that it would be kept in its natural state for everyone to enjoy.

The park was formally presented to the people of Leicestershire on 29 December 1928. A plaque of local granite inside the park records his generosity, and the Bradgate Park Trust he established still cares for it today. Bennion died just a few months later, in March 1929.

Civic life

Bennion served as a borough magistrate in Leicester, sat on the Court of Governors of the newly founded university college in 1922, and was a member of the Leicestershire Archaeological Society.
