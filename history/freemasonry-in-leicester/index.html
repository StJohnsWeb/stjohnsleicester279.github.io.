Freemasonry in Leicester: a history since 1739

From lodges meeting in Georgian inns on Gallowtree Gate and the Market Place to Freemasons' Hall on London Road, the story of the men who built Freemasonry across Leicester and the county.

Early lodges in Leicester

The first lodge of Freemasons in Leicester was established in 1739, just over twenty years after the Grand Lodge of England was formed in London. It did not last, and its warrant was eventually withdrawn. The town's Masons soon petitioned again, and in 1754 a new lodge was warranted to meet at the Pelican Inn on Gallowtree Gate.

A military lodge followed, attached to the Leicester Militia under the rival "Antients" Grand Lodge and taking its candidates only from the regiment. It is thought to have lapsed around 1764. None of these early lodges survived, which is why St John's, warranted in 1790, is the oldest lodge still meeting in the Province of Leicestershire and Rutland.

  1. 1739The first Leicester lodge is established. It later loses its warrant.
  2. 1754A new warrant is granted for a lodge at the Pelican Inn, Gallowtree Gate.
  3. 1790St John's Lodge is warranted and meets at the Lion and Dolphin, Market Place.
  4. 1813The Union of the Antients and Moderns creates the United Grand Lodge of England. For the next 33 years St John's is the only lodge working in Leicester.
  5. 1846John of Gaunt Lodge is consecrated as Leicester's second lodge.
  6. 1859Freemasons' Hall opens on Halford Street, funded by the Leicester lodges so they no longer need to meet in taverns.
  7. 1875Albert Edward Lodge is founded, one of a wave of new Leicester lodges in the late Victorian era.
  8. 1910St John's Lodge moves to Freemasons' Hall at 80 London Road.

Freemasons' Hall, London Road

Freemasons' Hall at 78–80 London Road brings together two early 19th century houses, both Grade II listed. Inside, the Holmes Lodge Room, designed by Leicester architect Howard H. Thompson, is regarded as one of the finest lodge rooms in the country. Today the hall is home to many of Leicester's lodges, including St John's.

Leaders of Leicestershire Freemasonry

The county's Freemasons are governed by the Provincial Grand Lodge of Leicestershire and Rutland, led by a Provincial Grand Master. Several of the early leaders were members of St John's.

Leicester Freemason

Sir Thomas Fowke

The first Provincial Grand Master of Leicestershire, and father of Sir Frederick Fowke.

Leicester Freemason

Lord Rancliffe

Provincial Grand Master of Leicestershire before 1851. The Rancliffe Lodge in Loughborough was named after him.

Leicester Freemason

Sir Frederick Gustavus Fowke, Bt

Initiated in 1813 in the Prince of Wales Lodge in London, he was appointed Senior Grand Warden of the Grand Lodge of England in 1821. He became Deputy Provincial Grand Master of Leicestershire in 1833 and Provincial Grand Master from 1851 until his death in 1856, and is credited with putting the Province on a lasting footing.

St John's Lodge

Earl Howe

A Worshipful Master of St John's who went on to serve as Provincial Grand Master until 1870.

St John's Lodge

William Kelly

Initiated into St John's in 1838. Deputy Provincial Grand Master from 1852, then Provincial Grand Master from 1870 until ill health led him to retire about three years later. He wrote the history of the Provincial Grand Lodge, and the William Kelly Charity Fund was named in his honour.

Leicester Freemason

S. S. Partridge

Initiated in John of Gaunt Lodge in 1866. As Provincial Grand Secretary for twelve years from 1872 he founded the Provincial Masonic Calendar, and he became Deputy Provincial Grand Master in 1884.

Leicester Freemason

Earl Ferrers

Provincial Grand Master of Leicestershire and Rutland at the turn of the 20th century. As he lived mostly abroad, his deputy ran the Province day to day.

Builders, merchants and benefactors

Leicester's Freemasons have included many of the men who shaped the city's industry and streetscape.

Leicester Freemason

William Millican

Architect, recorded in 1859 as a brother working on the new Masonic Hall at Leicester, and as Grand Superintendent of Works for the Province.

St John's Lodge

Charles Bennion

Founder of the British United Shoe Machinery Company and donor of Bradgate Park to the people of Leicestershire in 1928.

St John's Lodge

Sir Israel Hart

Four-time Mayor of Leicester and donor of the Town Hall Square fountain in 1879.

Researching Leicester Freemasonry

If you are tracing a Leicester ancestor who was a Freemason, the membership registers of the United Grand Lodge of England from 1751 to 1921 can be searched online through Ancestry. Masonic Periodicals Online holds free, searchable Victorian Masonic newspapers, which often reported Leicester installations by name. Locally, the Leicester Lodge of Research No. 2429 studies the history of the Craft in the county.
